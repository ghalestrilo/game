# Warlock Deck Strategies

A design brainstorm for warlock archetypes — what each strategy wants to do, what cards it needs, and what mechanics (existing or new) make it tick.

The warlock's distinction from the Mage and Sorcerer: power comes from a pact — with a demon, a great old one, an archfey. The patron grants immense power but extracts a cost. Every powerful card has a price: HP, cards, allies, or sanity. The warlock's identity is bargaining — more power than anyone, paid for in ways others refuse.

| Mechanic | Card | Notes |

## Strategy 1: Eldritch Patron (Pact resource engine)

**Concept:** The warlock has a `pact_charges` resource that regenerates each turn based on the patron's generosity. Every powerful card costs pact charges in addition to AP — or instead of AP. The warlock manages two resources simultaneously: AP (their own effort) and pact charges (borrowed power). The patron can grant bonus charges as a reward for risky play, or withhold them as punishment.

**Wants:**
- `pact_charges` as a secondary resource (starts at 3, regenerates 2 per turn)
- Cards that cost both AP and pact charges, or pact charges only
- Cards that grant bonus pact charges when played riskily (at low HP, against a tougher target)
- A patron "favor" system where risky play rewards more charges

**Key novel card ideas:**
- **Eldritch Blast** (1 AP, 1 charge) — Deal 7 damage. The warlock's signature spell — more efficient than Fireball per AP but costs a pact charge.
- **Dark Bargain** (0 AP, 3 charges) — Draw 3 cards. No AP cost — pure charge expenditure. The patron lends knowledge.
- **Patron's Wrath** (0 AP, 5 charges) — Deal 12 damage to all enemies. Free to cast with enough charges — but drains the reserve to zero.
- **Invoke Patron** (2 AP) — Gain 4 pact charges immediately. The recharge card — spend AP to refill the charge pool.
- **Desperate Plea** (0 AP, 0 charges) — Gain 5 pact charges, but lose 8 HP. Borrow charges from your own vitality when the patron won't give.

**New mechanic needed:** `pact_charges` as a secondary resource on the Character struct, regenerated by a fixed amount in `setup_player_turn`. Card `charge_cost` field alongside `ap` cost — deducted in `process_card`. Charge gate: if `pact_charges < charge_cost`, card cannot be played.

## Strategy 2: Hexblade (Melee curse striker)

**Concept:** The warlock fights in melee range, marking enemies with a Hexblade's Curse that amplifies all damage they take and heals the warlock when they die. Unlike most caster archetypes that stay at range, the Hexblade dives in, curses a target, and beats them down with augmented melee strikes. High risk, high sustain payoff.

**Wants:**
- Hexblade's Curse as a debuff that amplifies damage and grants life on kill
- Cheap melee strikes that deal bonus damage against cursed targets
- Cards that gap-close quickly (charge, dash)
- A kill-trigger that fires when a cursed target dies (heal, draw, or charge reset)

**Key novel card ideas:**
- **Hexblade's Curse** (1 AP) — Apply `hexbladed: 3` to target. While active, the warlock deals +3 damage against this target and gains 8 HP when this target dies.
- **Eldritch Smite** (1 AP) — Deal 5 damage. If target is `hexbladed`, deal 10 instead and apply `vulnerable: 1`.
- **Shadow Dash** (1 AP) — Move 3 tiles toward target. If target is `hexbladed`, this costs 0 AP.
- **Soul Leech** (2 AP) — Deal 6 damage to target. Heal for all damage dealt. If target is `hexbladed`, heal for 150% of damage dealt instead.
- **Curse Transfer** (1 AP) — Move `hexbladed` from target to another enemy. When the original target dies, the curse jumps — keeps the passive bonuses alive.

**Formula hook:** `target_is_hexbladed` alias uses `some` over `target.status_effects` for `type === "hexbladed"`. Kill-trigger on hexbladed target needs a hook in `check_target_death` that scans the caster's queue for the `hexbladed` buff and fires `apply_healing`. `Shadow Dash` AP override: a `free_if_hexbladed` cost formula reading `if(target_is_hexbladed, 0, 1)`.

## Strategy 3: Great Old One (Madness and mind control)

**Concept:** The warlock's patron is an incomprehensible alien entity. Cards apply `madness` to enemies — a stacking debuff that makes their actions increasingly erratic. At max madness stacks, the enemy is `dominated` and acts on the warlock's behalf for one turn. Pure psychological warfare — turn the most dangerous enemy into a temporary ally.

**Wants:**
- `madness` as a stacking status that builds toward domination
- Cards that apply madness efficiently (single-target and AoE)
- `dominated` as a temporary ally-flip status
- A payoff when domination ends (enemy takes penalty damage upon snapping out)

**Key novel card ideas:**
- **Whisper of Madness** (1 AP) — Apply `madness: 2` to target. Cheap and repeatable — the primary stacking tool.
- **Psychic Scream** (2 AP) — AoE: apply `madness: 1` to all enemies in range 2. Spread madness across the whole board.
- **Shatter Mind** (2 AP) — If target has 5+ `madness` stacks, apply `dominated: 1`. The dominated character acts on the warlock's team for 1 turn. Consumes all madness stacks.
- **Void Grasp** (1 AP) — Deal 3 damage. If target is `dominated`, they cannot be targeted by the warlock's own spells (prevent friendly-fire from a thrall).
- **Lingering Madness** (1 AP) — Prevent `madness` from decaying this turn on all affected enemies. Normally madness ticks down — this card holds it at current stacks.

**New mechanic needed:** `madness` stacking status with a tick-down per turn. `dominated` flips the character's `team` field to the warlock's team for 1 turn, then flips back — achieved with a `dominated` status that temporarily overwrites `team` in `setup_player_turn` and restores it when the status expires. This is the most complex single-status mechanic proposed across all docs — touching the turn queue, targeting validation, and team checks simultaneously.

## Strategy 4: Fiend Pact (Deal with devil — damage amplification)

**Concept:** The warlock sold their soul for pure damage output. Every card hits harder than it should. But each powerful card adds a `corruption` stack to the warlock — accumulating corruption reduces max HP permanently. Play too many big spells and the warlock destroys themselves. Manage corruption through cleanse cards and strategic restraint.

**Wants:**
- Corruption as a permanent (between turns) max HP reduction accumulator
- Cards that deal dramatically more damage than equivalent AP should allow
- Cleanse cards that remove corruption stacks
- A threshold where high corruption unlocks the most powerful cards (damage scales with how corrupted the warlock is)

**Key novel card ideas:**
- **Soul Burn** (2 AP) — Deal 16 damage. Apply `corruption: 2` to self (reduce max HP by 2 permanently until cleansed). The warlock's most efficient damage spell — extraordinary output at personal cost.
- **Devil's Bargain** (1 AP) — Apply `corruption: 1` to self. Draw 3 cards. Knowledge at a price — same philosophy as Blood Ritual but with permanent HP cost.
- **Hellfire** (3 AP) — Deal 12 AoE damage to all enemies. Apply `corruption: 3` to self. The nuclear option — massive board clear at heavy corruption cost.
- **Purge Corruption** (2 AP) — Remove all `corruption` stacks. Restore lost max HP. The only way to recover — expensive and uses an entire action.
- **Corrupt Ascendant** (2 AP) — Only playable at 5+ corruption stacks. Deal `corruption_stacks × 6` damage. The corruption payoff — highest damage output in the game, but requires being very close to self-destruction.

**New mechanic needed:** `corruption` as a permanent debuff that reduces `max_hp` — unlike `withered`, this persists across turns (not ticking down). Each stack permanently reduces `max_hp` by 1 until removed by `Purge Corruption`. This requires `max_hp` to be a mutable runtime value rather than read from `__constants__`, or tracking `corruption_penalty` separately and subtracting it in HP calculations.

## Strategy 5: Archfey Pact (Charm and escape)

**Concept:** The warlock's patron is capricious and beautiful — a fey lord who grants power over hearts and reality. Cards apply charm (enemies can't attack the warlock), blink (teleport anywhere on the board), and glamour (illusions that confuse targeting). The archfey warlock never takes a hit — they slide out of every dangerous situation with impossible grace.

**Wants:**
- Charm status (enemies can't target the warlock while charmed — they must target someone else)
- Blink (instant teleport to any unoccupied tile within range)
- Glamour/illusion effects (make enemies believe the warlock is elsewhere)
- Cards that reward avoiding damage entirely (bonus effects if the warlock took no damage this turn)

**Key novel card ideas:**
- **Fey Charm** (1 AP) — Apply `charmed: 2` to target. They cannot target the warlock for 2 turns. The core protection tool — one enemy neutralized each turn.
- **Misty Step** (1 AP) — Teleport to any unoccupied tile within range 4. Ignores all terrain and adjacency — pure blink. No movement cost, no attack-of-opportunity.
- **Glamour** (1 AP) — The warlock appears to be standing on an adjacent tile for 1 turn. All enemy targeting rules route to the glamour tile. The warlock's actual position is unaffected.
- **Fey Bargain** (2 AP) — Draw 3 cards. If the warlock took no damage last turn, also apply `charmed: 1` to all enemies. Rewards pristine play.
- **Summer Court Summons** (3 AP) — Summon an Archfey Knight (new unit: high HP, applies `charmed: 1` on every hit, acts as a distraction that all enemies want to attack over the warlock).

**Formula hook:** `warlock_took_no_damage_last_turn` alias checks `damage_taken_this_turn == 0` (shared counter from Paladin Judicator). `Misty Step` bypasses the normal movement validation entirely — a new `teleport` effect type that only checks if the destination tile is unoccupied, skipping range and terrain rules. `Glamour` needs a `glamour_position` field on the character that targeting rules read instead of `position` while the status is active.
